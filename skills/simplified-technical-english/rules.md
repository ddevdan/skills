# ASD-STE100 Writing Rules

The rules the STE controlled language applies to prose. Rule numbers follow the ASD-STE100 specification; the official spec and Dictionary are the authority — request the free copy from https://www.asd-ste100.org/. Apply every rule below to every sentence you rewrite.

## Words

- **1.1 — Use approved words only.** Choose words from the approved STE Dictionary, approved Technical Names, or approved Technical Verbs. Check `dictionary.csv` first and take the listed approved alternative. When a word is not in the bundled list, prefer the plainest common synonym and flag it for review.
- **1.2 — One part of speech per word.** Use an approved word only as the part of speech the Dictionary gives it. Do not use the same word as both a noun and a verb.
- **1.3 — One meaning per word.** Keep to a word's single approved meaning. "Follow" means "come after", not "obey" — write "obey the safety instructions".
- **1.4 — Use approved Technical Names.** Use the correct, consistent name for each part, system, or tool. Do not vary it.
- Do not use synonyms. Say the same thing the same way every time.

## Nouns

- **2.1 — No noun cluster longer than three nouns.** Break long strings of nouns with prepositions or hyphens. "runway light control system" → "control system for the runway lights".

## Verbs

- **3.x — Use only the approved verb forms:** the infinitive, the imperative (commands), the simple present, the simple past, and the simple future.
- **Use the active voice.** Always in procedures ("Open the valve"), and as much as possible in descriptive writing. The active voice names who does the action.
- **Do not use the "-ing" form** (gerund or present participle) unless it is part of an approved Technical Name. "Before starting the engine" → "Before you start the engine".
- **Do not use complex or compound tenses** (no present perfect, no continuous, no "shall have been"). "We have removed the panel" → "We removed the panel".
- **Use the simple past, not the present perfect**, to describe a completed action.
- **Do not use the subjunctive.**

## Sentences

- **Keep procedure sentences to 20 words or fewer.**
- **Keep descriptive sentences to 25 words or fewer.**
- **One instruction per sentence** in a procedure. Split combined steps: "Open the panel and remove the filter" → two sentences, or a numbered list.
- **One topic per sentence.**
- **Start the sentence with its main topic**, then give the detail.
- **Use articles** ("the", "a", "an") wherever possible — do not drop them for brevity.
- **Use a vertical list** for complex conditions or a sequence of steps, instead of one long sentence.
- **Use simple connecting words** ("and", "or", "then", "but") to link related ideas; do not stack clauses.

## Paragraphs

- **6.7 — A paragraph has 6 sentences at most.** Do not use single-sentence paragraphs more than once per 10 paragraphs.
- **Start each paragraph with a topic sentence.**
- **One topic per paragraph.**

## Warnings, cautions, and notes

- **Put the warning or caution before the step it applies to**, never after.
- **Start a warning or caution with a clear command** ("Do not touch the hot surface"), then say why.
- **Be specific** about the hazard and the consequence.
- **Notes** give information only. A note must not contain an instruction.

## Punctuation

- **8.11 — Do not use semicolons.** Write two short sentences instead.
- Do not use slashes to mean "and/or" — choose the word you mean.
- Use hyphens to join related words and to avoid long noun clusters.
- Keep punctuation simple; avoid parentheses inside instructions.
